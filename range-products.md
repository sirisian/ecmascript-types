# Multiplicative numeric interval arithmetic

Built-in range `*` and `/` compute coordinate enclosures with one agreed numeric endpoint type. They use exact products or quotients, select mathematical extrema, and only then represent and admit the result bounds. They do not narrow through Number or construct a rounded reciprocal. The domain and admission rules are shared with [addition](range-addition.md); this document makes the nonlinear choices explicit.

## Requirements and scope

A valid direction must preserve wide values, a predictable endpoint domain, and an enclosure of the exact coordinate calculation. It must account for every sign region, open bounds, constant images attained by interior points, emptiness, missing bounds and explicit infinities. It must specify failures and effects without changing reference authority or liveness. A range denotes a coordinate interval here, not an enumeration of representable members: the existing `isEmpty` judgment and continuous bound algebra apply, independently of the integer lattice used by `length` or iteration. Multiplying a range by itself supplies two independent operands; it is not the tighter image of the single-variable function `x => x*x`.

Inspect both domains and every present endpoint before any empty or singular exit. All contributions must be the same supported concrete numeric type, including metadata. A union is not an arithmetic carrier. An erased `any` contributes its actual endpoints; full-any contributes nothing. The agreed domain is retained, or remains `any` when both operands contribute nothing. This rule is independent of selected corners and magnitudes. Invalid kinds, domain disagreement, or unordered endpoints throw TypeError without invoking user comparisons, coercions or public endpoint getters. Partial-interface sketches in `ranges.md` do not by themselves certify arbitrary class arithmetic as an enclosure algorithm.

## Exact extrema and representation

| Direction | Ergonomics / autocomplete / wrong guess | Prior art, including Rust | Production performance, shapes and ICs | Correctness and liveness | Other / decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scalar wrapping, truncating division, or nearest rounding | Looks familiar but can shrink or reverse bounds. | Rust integer division is scalar division; checked division checks zero/overflow, not interval enclosure. | Cheap instructions cannot justify a wrong enclosure. | Excludes exact results. | **Reject.** |
| Promote integer quotients to rational, or outward-round them to integers | Fewer errors, but changes the domain or adds a second integer representability policy. | Rust uses explicit conversion; Boost supports explicit rounding policies. Neither dictates integer promotion here. | More carrier/rounding branches and polymorphic outputs. | Promotion violates the selected domain; integer widening would conflict with scaling's exact integer rule. | **Reject implicit policies.** |
| Represent and admit each reciprocal/corner before selecting extrema | Reuses scalar paths, but can fail on an irrelevant fraction or admit up to eight intermediate bounds. | Boost's inclusion principle requires sound composed rounding; it does not require this proposal to expose intermediates. Rust has no such hook contract. | Extra divisions, allocation, rounding and user calls. | `Range<uint8>(2)/Range<uint8>(2)` must produce 1 without trying to store 1/2 in uint8. | **Reject.** |
| Exact corner products/quotients; represent only final lower and upper extrema | Stable domain and explicit failure if an exact integer/rational bound cannot fit. Floating bounds may widen. | Rust checked arithmetic motivates explicit failure; Boost's directed enclosure is the closer numerical analogy. | Four exact candidate calculations and comparisons; native checked/directed fast paths when equivalent, big-integer fallback by operand size. No member enumeration or shape mutation. | Reuses scaling's representability and admission invariants. | **Recommend.** |

For finite coordinates, multiplication selects the least and greatest of the four products; division selects the least and greatest of the four exact quotients. Denominator sign is normalized before comparison. No finite candidate is rounded or admitted before selection. Integer results must be integral and fit; rational results reduce before their width check. Binary and decimal bounds round outward, and moved bounds close. Finite overflow, binary subnormals, and the supported decimal adjusted-exponent floor follow [range-scaling.md](range-scaling.md). Only the selected bounds need representation. Decimal results use reduced coefficient/exponent form, including multiplication or division by one; these operators have no identity-copy admission exemption.

For an exact finite nonzero extreme, a closed attaining corner closes the result; otherwise it is open. But corner closure alone is insufficient for constant images. If either multiplicand contains zero, a zero extreme is attained even when the other range's endpoints are both open. In division, a zero numerator can attain zero through an interior divisor; a closed explicit infinite divisor also yields zero for any finite numerator. A closed explicit infinite multiplicand yields its signed infinity through any suitably signed nonzero value of the other operand, including an interior one. An explicit infinite numerator similarly yields infinity through a finite nonzero divisor. Thus `(0..=0)*(0<..<1)` is closed zero, and `(Infinity..=Infinity)*(1<..<2)` is a closed infinity singleton.

Always retaining corner openness is rejected: it turns nonempty constant images into empty ranges. Closing every result is also rejected: it discards meaningful exact exclusions. The selected attainment rules preserve familiar point-image expectations, add fixed sign/presence tests without shape or IC effects, and require no reference access. Rust's contains/is_empty distinction helps with point membership but supplies no range arithmetic; Boost's always-closed intervals cannot prescribe this open-bound policy.

Binary candidate zero signs follow scalar multiplication/division signs, including signs of infinite coordinates. On a tie at exact zero, choose negative zero below if any tied candidate has that sign, and positive zero above if any has positive sign; otherwise retain the sole sign. This is symmetric under exchanging product operands. Always canonicalizing zero would lose an observable scalar sign; retaining the first candidate would make ties depend on corner enumeration. Both are rejected. The selected constant-cost sign rule preserves enclosure and type/liveness invariants. Rust floating signed zero is a representation analogy, not an interval tie rule. Decimal zero remains canonical. Rounding a nonzero finite value to binary zero retains the exact value's sign.

## Missing bounds and explicit infinities

| Direction | Ergonomics / autocomplete / wrong guess | Prior art, including Rust | Production performance, shapes and ICs | Correctness and liveness | Other / decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fall back to full for every partially bounded product | Simple, but loses known sign bounds such as negative times negative. | Rust has one-ended ranges but no product rule. | Cheap; pushes avoidable checks downstream. | Conservative but unnecessarily weak. | **Reject.** |
| Replace missing endpoints by stored infinity values | Convenient algebra; changes reflection and cannot inhabit integer/rational domains. | Boost's infinite real-set limits differ from observable infinity endpoints here. | Introduces values and admissions for absent fields. | Violates presence and endpoint-domain rules. | **Reject.** |
| Treat missing endpoints as private signed limits, retaining absence provenance | Predictable sign bounds and unchanged public shape meaning. | Extends the interval-library limit idea while preserving the proposal's explicit/absent distinction. Rust supplies no arithmetic precedent. | Fixed two-edge records, signs and presence bits; optimizable away on bounded paths. | Sound conservative bounds without manufacturing an unrepresentable infinity. | **Recommend.** |

For candidate calculation, a missing lower/upper bound is a private negative/positive infinite limit, marked absent and not attained. A nonzero product with such a limit is an infinite candidate with absence provenance; any infinite contributor with absence gives that candidate absence. Zero times a missing limit contributes a zero limit with the multiplication sign. A finite numerator divided by an infinite coordinate contributes signed zero. A missing numerator limit divided by an explicit infinite divisor also contributes zero; an infinite numerator coordinate divided by a missing divisor limit contributes no candidate. The other corners determine the enclosure in that last case: `(2..)/(2..)` has an open zero lower bound and no upper bound. A divisor passing the nonzero guard always supplies enough other candidates.

At a tied infinite extreme, combine absence provenance. A negative-infinite lower extreme or positive-infinite upper extreme with that provenance remains absent. In the opposite roles, retain a present infinite bound: a positive-infinite lower bound or negative-infinite upper bound still constrains the result to that extreme. A present source infinity necessarily established these cases. Thus `(Infinity..=Infinity)*(2..)` yields a closed-infinity lower bound with no upper bound, rather than losing both constraints. Otherwise construct the present signed infinity and determine attainment as above. Finite zero limits remain present numeric zero and close only when attained or moved by rounding. No missing field receives admission. These calculations do not enumerate implicit infinite inhabitants of a missing-bound range. For example, `(0..=0)*(..)` is closed zero, and `(..)/(Infinity..=Infinity)` is closed zero under this bound-information contract. This is a conservative coordinate-bound algebra, not a complete algebra of all subsets of IEEE values.

| Indeterminate explicit-coordinate direction | Ergonomics / autocomplete / wrong guess | Prior art, including Rust | Production performance, shapes and ICs | Correctness and liveness | Other / decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Publish NaN, drop the offending bound, or choose an infinity | Appears successful but loses the cause of an invalid calculation. | Rust scalar floats can yield NaN; NaN cannot be an ordered endpoint here. | Cheap but unusable result semantics. | NaN violates ordering; arbitrary loss/choice violates specified failure behavior. | **Reject.** |
| Discard undefined pairs and enclose only defined products/quotients, considering endpoint exclusion | More finite-limit programs succeed, but an invalid subset of operand pairs silently disappears. | Boost's real-set intervals motivate limits; they do not model this proposal's observable included infinity singletons. Rust offers no corresponding set operator. | Sign/definedness branches remain bounded but need a distinct domain-of-definition contract. | Can be made sound for a partial-image API, but would differ from the additive rejection of explicit indeterminate bounds. | **Reject for these operators.** Such an API should state its partial-image policy. |
| Reject explicit zero/infinity indeterminacy before representation | Wrong infinity guesses fail visibly, including excluded explicit boundary coordinates. Missing bounds remain the spelling for absence of a constraint. | Uses checked failure and the existing additive policy; no claim that Rust or Boost mandates it. | Fixed sign/presence tests, no endpoint enumeration or rollback. | Maintains ordered results and consistent explicit-versus-missing semantics. | **Recommend.** |

After validation/empty/singularity exits, multiplication throws RangeError if either range has a present infinite endpoint and the other contains zero or has a zero endpoint. This includes an excluded zero or infinity endpoint and a zero interior. Division throws RangeError if both ranges have present infinite endpoints, including excluded ones. Do this preflight before admitting either result bound. For example, `(0<..=1)*(1..<Infinity)` rejects; spelling the missing upper bound `(1..)` instead allows the limit calculation. Explicit finite-over-infinity and infinity-over-finite calculations remain supported. Empty operands avoid all these irrelevant calculations.

## Divisor approaching zero

| Direction | Ergonomics / autocomplete / wrong guess | Prior art, including Rust | Production performance, shapes and ICs | Correctness and liveness | Other / decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Throw whenever the divisor is not bounded away from zero | Catches singular inputs, but breaks the documented conservative result used by bound propagation. | Rust checked scalar division rejects a zero value; a set approaching zero is not that value. | Simple guard; new exception edges for existing programs. | Sound failure, inconsistent with the established interval contract. | **Reject.** |
| Always return a tight connected hull of defined quotients | Better bounds for `0<..=1`, but needs an explicit policy for division by zero and for discarding undefined pairs. | Boost supports a tighter hull and uses empty for an exactly-zero divisor. Rust has no interval division rule. | A bounded sign case table is feasible; cost is not the reason for rejection. | Sound under a revised partial-image contract, but incompatible with the current uniform singularity rule. | **Reject for this operator contract.** |
| Return two intervals or an interval union | Preserves gaps for divisors crossing zero; changes every consumer's result type and autocomplete. | Boost exposes separate split-division functions; Rust's Range is not a union of intervals. | New result representations, allocations and polymorphic consumer paths. | Expressive, but violates the single RangeBounds result promise. | **Reject implicit union results.** |
| Keep full-range fallback unless the lower bound is strictly positive or upper strictly negative | Less precise near zero, but one result interface and the documented conservative outcome. | Existing proposal precedent; explicitly differs from Boost's finer singularity policy. | Two sign comparisons and existing full-range shape. | Preserves the established contract and agreed domain without evaluating a zero quotient. | **Recommend.** A separately named tighter/split operation can declare different semantics. |

An open zero endpoint is not bounded away from zero. The fallback also applies to a zero singleton or a zero numerator divided by an approaching-zero divisor. Domain validation and empty-image handling precede this rule; the fallback retains the agreed domain and performs no endpoint admission.

## Effects, failures and publication

Copy the first empty operand, left preferred, with the agreed domain and no readmission. Manufacturing an unrelated empty zero anchor is rejected because metadata can forbid it; computing crossed empty endpoints is rejected because it can create a nonempty result or irrelevant overflow. Rust's range emptiness and the mathematical empty image are useful analogies; the chosen representative is proposal-specific. Fixed internal checks preserve source shapes, endpoint payloads and liveness.

Perform domain validation, empty selection, division singularity handling, indeterminate preflight and exact candidate selection in that order. Then represent/admit the lower result and only afterward represent/admit the upper. Publish last. Computing/admitting every corner is rejected because callers would observe unused computations; representing both final bounds before either admission is rejected because an upper failure would suppress required lower effects. Interleaving callbacks with extremum search is rejected because hook effects would depend on optimization or corner enumeration. The chosen order has at most two ordinary admissions, no rollback journal or IC invalidation, and an explicit first error. Rust has no arbitrary numeric-metadata hook analogue; the proposal's checked boundary is the controlling prior rule.

Metadata admission must preserve value, binary zero sign and decimal cohort, propagating hook errors or throwing RangeError on representation change. It may attach the promised metadata. Earlier hook effects are not rolled back, neither input is mutated, and no partial result is published. A subsequent call can reuse the source after failure. Operand expressions still evaluate in ordinary source order before dispatch. Success, failure, full fallback and empty copies grant no reference authority and do not revive stale aliases.

## Remaining scope

The implementation and normative clause are `range-products.mts` and `sec-multiplicative-range-arithmetic`. Static range-result typing, general custom enclosure declarations, extended-family iteration and the mixed plain BigInt construction restriction remain separate work. Passing bounded tests does not close the wider checking/inference gates.

Primary sources: [Rust Range](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/ops/struct.Range.html), [Rust checked multiplication and division](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/primitive.i32.html#method.checked_mul), and [Boost interval arithmetic](https://www.boost.org/doc/libs/latest/libs/numeric/interval/doc/interval.htm). These sources support the limited analogies in the tables; the proposal's open bounds, explicit infinity values and metadata admissions require their own rules.
